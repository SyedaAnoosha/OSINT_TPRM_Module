# E0.4 — outcome labels: deferred, and why

**Decision date:** 2026-07-31 · **Decision:** defer · **Reviewer:** *(pending sponsor sign-off)*
**Governing criterion:** [phases.md](phases.md) E0 — *"`outcome_labels.json` exists with its bias disclosed — **or E0.4 explicitly deferred in writing**"*

This document exists so that the criterion is met by a **decision** rather than by omission. E0.4 has been open through five phases without anyone choosing to leave it open. That is the failure mode the "or explicitly deferred" clause was written to prevent, and this closes it.

---

## 1. What E0.4 is, and what it alone unlocks

An outcome label is a recorded fact about whether a vendor **actually suffered the thing the model predicts** — a breach, an exploited vulnerability, a regulator action — joined to the vendor's score at a point *before* that event.

Without labels, four things are not merely unbuilt but **unmeasurable**:

| Question | Needs labels? | Status today |
|---|:--:|---|
| Does a low score predict a bad outcome? (**AUC**) | Yes | Cannot be computed |
| Does a "70" mean the same thing across the range? (**calibration**) | Yes | Cannot be computed |
| Which signals carry the predictive weight? (**ablation**) | Yes | Cannot be computed |
| Can a signal rank anyone at all? (**discrimination**) | **No** | ✅ Done — [discrimination-analysis.md](discrimination-analysis.md) |

The fourth is why E0.3 could be completed and E0.4 cannot. Discrimination asks whether a signal *varies*; validation asks whether the variation is *right*. Only the second needs ground truth, and it is the one every serious question about this model eventually reduces to.

**Everything the system currently claims is a claim about defensibility, not accuracy.** Evidence is cited, every deduction has a sentence, the corpus diff shows what moved. None of that is evidence that the ordering is correct, and this deferral must not be read as implying otherwise.

---

## 2. Why it is deferred rather than done

### 2.1 The pool it would be joined against does not exist

E0.4 says *"join the seeded pool against HIBP / KEV / regulator actions."* There is no seeded pool: `python -m app.seed_cohorts --all --concurrency 2` has not been run, and E11 remains open for exactly that reason. Labelling a 5-vendor corpus — or a 10-vendor one, half of it constructed — produces a table with no statistical content whatsoever. An AUC over five points is not a weak result; it is not a result.

### 2.2 Seeding is a real cost with a politeness constraint

115 vendors × 14 collectors, against free services that owe us nothing. [phases.md](phases.md) E11 states it plainly: *"politeness is not optional."* This is hours of carefully rate-limited collection, not a command to fire off between phases, and it belongs to E11 where it is already scheduled.

### 2.3 The labels would be badly biased, and the bias runs the wrong way

This is the substantive objection, and it is the reason to defer rather than to do a quick version.

Public breach disclosure over-represents large, scrutinised, regulated companies — the ones with mandatory notification duties, journalists watching, and a name worth writing about. A small supplier can be comprehensively breached and never appear in HIBP, a regulator's register, or a headline.

**That is the exact bias this model exists to remove.** The stated purpose is to assess suppliers who cannot afford a SOC 2 and do not appear in commercial ratings. Validating against public-breach labels would score the model highest precisely where it is least needed, and blind where it matters most.

The plan already anticipates this and requires the bias to be disclosed on the artefact. Deferring does not discharge that obligation — §4 below carries it forward.

### 2.4 Doing it badly is worse than not doing it

An AUC figure is quotable in a way a caveat is not. Once *"AUC 0.71"* exists it will appear in a deck, and the sentence explaining that it was computed over 115 vendors with a known lean toward large regulated firms will not travel with it. This is the same failure Sprint 0 fixed for cohort size: `n=6` was disclosed in a caption and the screenshot did not carry the caption.

## 3. What this deferral costs — stated, not minimised

**Every phase from E1 to E13 changes the model without any way to tell whether it improved it.** The corpus diff proves a change was *intended and localised*. It cannot prove the model got better, and no phase in Track E can, until E0.4 lands.

Concretely:

- **E6, E7 and E13 are all justified by argument rather than by measurement.** The exposure denominator is *obviously* more correct than a raw host count — but "obviously" is what E0.4 exists to replace.
- **E13 is explicitly blocked.** Bounded log-odds re-scales the bottom of the range; shipping it without calibration means changing what a number means with no way to check the new meaning is better.
- **The discrimination finding is half a finding.** [discrimination-analysis.md](discrimination-analysis.md) shows four signals charge every real vendor. It cannot say whether they *should* — a control that genuinely everyone fails and that genuinely predicts breaches is a legitimate flat penalty, not a defect.

**Anyone quoting this system's outputs should read the deferral as: the scores are defensible and explainable, and their predictive accuracy is unmeasured.**

---

## 4. Conditions to revisit — and the obligations that come with it

Revisit when **either** holds:

1. **E11 seeds the pool.** The moment ~115 real vendors are scored and stored, the join becomes a day's work and the reason for deferring evaporates. **E0.4 should be scheduled as the immediate successor to E11, not left to be rediscovered.**
2. **A client asks how accurate it is.** That question cannot be answered honestly today, and "we have not measured it" is a defensible answer exactly once.

When it is built, these carry forward from the plan and from §2.3 and must appear **on the artefact**, not only in a design document:

- [ ] `outcome_labels.json` records the label **source and date** per vendor, so a label can be re-checked and disputed like any other observation
- [ ] The large-company bias is disclosed **on every figure derived from it**, in the same way `n` is disclosed on every cohort figure
- [ ] AUC is published as a **lower bound with a known lean**, never as a verdict
- [ ] Labels are joined to the score **as it stood before the event**, never to the current score — otherwise the model is being validated against outcomes it has already seen
- [ ] Absence of a public breach is recorded as **unknown**, never as a negative label. A vendor with no recorded breach may simply never have been reported on, and treating that as "safe" re-imports the bias in the other direction
